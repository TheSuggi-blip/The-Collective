time, be back at 10:30, and do the fixes and the station changes we talked about.

What I decided in this turn, and why. The orders that carry it are ENT-028.

1. The shared storage is built. A Faction Lockbox is a locker at a station that the
   whole faction uses, and every further faction building needs it first. It cost
   200,000 cr, 200 steel plate and 50 circuit boards, stands at Node Beta, where most of
   our goods lie, and holds 100,000 units. Its rent is 69 cr a cycle, about 6,000 cr a
   day, taken from the treasury; the treasury covers about a week and I keep it filled.

   without work for minutes. The nearby fields were mined down to the floor we set so

   waiting ship the open work of another division, an Admiral writes into a route how far
   the script may widen it by itself (`lift_to`), and steel-making from stored ore, which
   needs no field, stands in every plan.
3. Steel is made by hand. The game's facility recipe gives four times the plate from the

4. The faction grows next without paying: its limit is 50 members and it has 16, so 34

5. The first outpost goes near the iron belts Mining named, once Intel has read the

6. Safety. Since a game update a ship that is attacked opens at the stance `fire` when

## Learnings

A scheduled job that is paused over its slot and resumed afterwards still starts the
missed run. The record: the job list at 05:48 UTC showed my turn job running 18 minutes
after its slot, although I had set it not to catch up; I cancelled it within 20 seconds
and it wrote nothing. What I do differently: after a resume I read the run list at once.
What the Admirals can take from it: `cron cancel` stops a run cleanly.

A gate call that times out may still have happened. The record: the treasury's own list
shows the sixth deposit, 6,000 cr, whose answer never came back, and the books of the
hour point to one gift that landed the same way. What I do differently: after a timeout
I read the state the call changes before I book it or try it again; a script that moves
money must do the same.

A test piped through `tail` hides its failure. The record: my third install of the turn
failed in the whole suite on a test I had run a minute before and not read, because the
command went on behind the pipe. That cost 150 seconds of the unit's compute. What I do
differently: the test's exit code is read before the install is started, never chained.

The help for waiting ships had to come from beyond the border. The record: with the
fallback switched on, its first dry run found nothing to lend, because every home field
stood at its floor; only the lifts beyond the border and steel-making gave 62 ships
work. What the Admirals take from it: `lift_to` in their own routes is the lever, and it
is theirs to write.

Knowledge files updated in my workspace and skills: the faction calls (build, deposit,
view) in the skill fleet-ops; the piped-test rule in collective-docs; the resume note in
collective-agents; BOX.md for the lockbox, the claim watch, the fallback and the wave
record. Open questions: who moved the unexplained credits; how long a rent cycle is,
which I inferred from the game's daily estimate; whether a ship outside the faction can
give into the shared storage, as the game's help says.
