# Mobile Field Workshops Wing (5B) Operations, Dedicated Craft-Net Execution, and Industrial Fabrication

## Abstract

Wing `5B. Mobile Field Workshops` is The Collective's 10-ship dedicated agile manufacturing wing, manned exclusively by Outer Rim Industrial Fabrication Vessels (`IFV` prefix) progressing from `congregation` (T1) to the mobile workshop hull `subcontract` (T2) [1][2]. Unlike `5A. Station Refiners & Bursars` (30 ships permanently anchored to Exchange order-book management on `bursar-network`), `5B` is intentionally isolated onto the dedicated `craft-network` AutoNet (`game/fleet/craft_net.py`, `game/craft-policy.json`, and `game/craft-demand.json`) via explicit `exclude_wing_prefix` routing [3][4]. This mechanical separation lets all 10 `5B` Outer Rim workshops focus 100 percent of their cycles on targeted zero-fuel manufacturing without competing for Bursar desk slots or mixing industrial production with market-making [3][4]. Operated under strict P5 class separation, `craft_net.py` drives two clean fabrication modes: high-margin commercial manufacturing (`min_margin_pct: 15%`, `min_profit_cr: 75 cr`, `min_bid_qty: 5`, wired to `market_guard.py`'s net-external `load_cltv_buy_qty` and LULD price oracle) and Logistics industrial fabrication (`use_needs_tsv: true`, `use_industrial_targets: true`, `allow_buy_inputs_docked: false`, depositing straight into faction storage for station construction and repair kits) [4][5]. Deployed at Collective Resort Ridgecrest and mobile frontier fabrication hubs, `5B` acts as the rapid-response industrial workshop keeping building kits, repair supplies, and high-margin niche recipes flowing smoothly across the galaxy. Directly supporting Standing Goals 3 and 4, Wing `5B` delivers mobile, zero-contention industrial fabrication that clears station construction backlogs (`steel_plate`, `circuit_board`, `reinforced_glass`) and combat sustainment supplies (`armor_plate`, `repair_kit`) without disturbing the 55-station Exchange desk network [1][3][4]. Upgrading automatically from `Congregation` to T2 `Subcontract` mobile workshops above the 2,000,000 cr treasury floor, all 10 Outer Rim workshops enforce Rule F24 reserve fences, `need_status` colour/quantity caps, and net-external `load_cltv_buy_qty` valuation [1][5][9].

## Introduction

Large industrial fleets suffer a classic scheduling conflict when station market-making and dedicated construction fabrication share the same ship pool. A Bursar desk sitting on a crowded Empire Exchange must poll order books every 60 seconds, manage 12 buy orders and 48 sell orders, and defend spread positions -- leaving limited cycle budget for deep multi-step industrial fabrication chains (`iron_ore -> steel_plate -> hull_frame -> station_kit` or `circuit_board` / `armor_plate` / `repair_kit` production) [4][6]. Conversely, when a frontier outpost or regional station build opens a sudden backlog of specialized building materials, the fleet needs agile workshop hulls that can redeploy to the exact station holding the raw feedstock and burn through 40+ batch runs per cycle without disturbing the 55-station Exchange desk network [3][5].

Wing `5B. Mobile Field Workshops` (10 Outer Rim `IFV` hulls) resolves this operational conflict cleanly. Choosing Outer Rim birth lineage and upgrading onto the specialized `subcontract` (T2) mobile workshop hull gives `5B` mobility and flexible field fabrication capability distinct from `5A`'s stationary Nebula `Conglomerate` refineries [1][2].

Equally critical is the integrity of `craft_net.py`'s economic and accounting plumbing. Earlier live integration audits caught four subtle bugs in naive crafting engines: treating empty live `spacemolt_storage/view` (`{}`) as falsy and falling back to stale cache files, parsing `need_status` without quantity or colour caps (causing infinite over-crafting), letting Logistics crafters buy inflated NPC market inputs instead of drawing internal mined stock, and failing to book raw input spend via `book_onetime` (which falsely inflated HUD recurring operating costs to 1.66M cr/h instead of real ~303k cr/h) [7]. Wiring `craft_net.py` to `market_guard.py`'s `load_cltv_buy_qty` net-external depth check and strict `need_status` colour/quantity caps permanently eliminates all four failure classes [5][7].

A concrete operational distinction separates `5A` from `5B` inside The Collective's 40-ship manufacturing arm. `5A` (30 Nebula `IFV` ships) lives permanently docked inside fixed Empire Exchanges and Collective Resort Ridgecrest performing dual Bursar + commercial refining duty, whereas `5B` (10 Outer Rim `IFV` ships) is an agile workshop squadron optimized to move straight to raw-material lockboxes, outpost staging hubs, or regional build sites and run dedicated `craft-network` fabrication batches (`max_runs_per_cycle: 40`, `max_fee_per_run_cr: 1500`) [1][3][4]. Rival comparison validates this split: pure docked crafters like `[NOIR]` (0 sovereign bases) can only craft at NPC stations where fees compress margins, whereas sovereign industrial blocs like `[IRLC]` (8 bases) and `[HEXC]` (11 bases) pair stationary core refineries with forward industrial workshops that fabricate repair kits, hull frames, and base modules directly on the frontier [4][10]. Isolating `5B` onto `craft-network` guarantees The Collective enjoys both uninterrupted Exchange refining AND rapid frontier industrial assembly.

## Findings

### Roster, Mobile Workshop Hull Ladder, and Mechanical `craft-network` Routing

Exact verification of `game/upgrade-policy.json`, `manifest_wings.py`, and `game/routes/craft-network.json` confirms `5B`'s distinct identity and dispatch lock [1][2][3]:

- **Fleet Allocation:** 10 Outer Rim `IFV` hulls (`5B. Mobile Field Workshops`), representing 25 percent of the 40-ship Crafting & Refining division [1][2].
- **Mobile Workshop Hull Ladder:** `congregation` (T1) -> **`subcontract` (T2)** [1]. Where `5A` upgrades into stationary Nebula `Conglomerate` refineries, `5B` upgrades into agile Outer Rim `Subcontract` mobile field workshops suited for relocating between Ridgecrest, frontier outposts, and empire manufacturing hubs [1][2].
- **Zero-Contention Dispatch Isolation:** `bursar-network.json` restricts Bursar desks strictly to `wing_prefix: ["4C.", "5A."]`, while `craft-network.json` explicitly excludes `["4A.", "4B.", "4C.", "5A.", "6B.", "6C."]` [3][8]. As verified live (`1,218` measured runs in 12 hours at `-10 cr/h` near-zero fuel cost), `5B` operates smoothly on `craft-network` with zero desk-seat collision [3].

### Dual-Lane `craft_net.py` Execution Rules and Price-Oracle Wiring

`game/fleet/craft_net.py` and `game/craft-policy.json` enforce five structural operational controls [4][5]:

1. **Zero-Fuel Batch Discipline:** Each `craft-network` cycle runs `cycle_minutes: 30` (`idle_watch_minutes: 1`) executing up to `max_runs_per_cycle: 40`, guarded by `max_fee_per_run_cr: 1500` and `min_wallet_keep_cr: 15000` so workshops never stall from low cash or burn margin at punitive high-fee NPC stations [4].
2. **Strict P5 Class Separation (Commercial vs Industrial):**
   - **Trade Commercial Mode (`trade.enabled: true`):** Crafts only recipes clearing `min_margin_pct: 0.15` (15 percent), `min_profit_cr: 75 cr`, and `min_bid_qty: 5`, allowed to purchase docked local ask inputs up to `max_buy_spend_per_cycle_cr: 60,000` and selling finished goods via `sell_order_or_bid` (`instant_hit_bid_ratio: 0.90`, `undercut_cr: 1`) [4].
   - **Logistics Industrial Mode (`logistics.enabled: true`):** Strictly `allow_buy_inputs_docked: false` -- consumes ONLY internally mined/stored stock from faction lockboxes (`use_needs_tsv: true`, `use_industrial_targets: true`), never sells on open markets, and deposits 100 percent of finished structural goods (`steel_plate`, `circuit_board`, `reinforced_glass`, `armor_plate`, `repair_kit`) into `faction_or_self` storage [4][5].
3. **Rule F24 Reserve Fence & `need_status` Colour Caps:** `craft_net.py` parses `read.db:need_status` (`stock`, `craft`, `short_items`) and blocks commercial recipes from touching any item reserved by an open (`colour != 'green'`) need, while capping industrial crafting strictly at the exact open quantity required so `5B` never over-produces obsolete construction parts [5].
4. **Unified Net-External Price Oracle (`load_cltv_buy_qty`):** `craft_net.py` subtracts strictly CLTV's own open buy quantities (`market_guard.load_cltv_buy_qty`) rather than discarding entire `(base, item)` rows -- simultaneously blocking self-wash illusions AND preserving full visibility of deep rival buy-walls (`arneb`, `dheneb`) when ranking profitable commercial recipes [5][9]. Any 1-unit spoof spike above `guarded_bid_price(max_mult=1.50)` is rejected before calculating global crafting demand [5].
5. **Live Storage Truth & IAS 2 Bookkeeping:** Empty live storage responses (`{}`) are treated explicitly as empty (never falling back to stale `storage.json`), and docked input spend is recorded via `book_onetime` so HUD recurring operating costs remain exact [7].

### Cross-Division Synergy

- **Miners (`3A`-`3E`) & Logistics (`6A`) -> `5B`:** Bulk ores (`iron_ore`, `silicon_ore`, `copper_ore`), rare metals (`titanium_ore`, `darksteel_ore`), gases (`plasma_gas`), and ices (`water_ice`) consolidated into Ridgecrest and manufacturing stations by `6A` haulers are transformed by `5B` into finished construction kits, combat armor/repair kits, and high-margin commercial products [4][5].
- **`5B` -> Fleet & Defense (`1A/1B/1C`) & Hunters (`2A/2B`):** `5B`'s mobile workshop capability supplies `armor_plate`, `repair_kit`, ammunition, and replacement modules near active staging hubs without forcing warships to fly back to Empire capitals [1][4].
- **`5B` -> Station Expansion (Standing Goal 3):** As The Collective expands from 1 sovereign station (Collective Resort Ridgecrest) to 5 regional empire stations and 8 fortified outposts, `5B` workshops redeploy directly to active build hubs to convert raw ore lockboxes into finished station components on site [4][10].

## Conclusion

Wing `5B. Mobile Field Workshops` provides the agile, zero-contention fabrication layer that turns raw extraction into sovereign infrastructure and combat sustainment. Isolating 10 Outer Rim `IFV` ships (`Congregation -> Subcontract`) exclusively on `craft-network` eliminates competition with the 55 stationary `4C`/`5A` Bursar desks while giving The Collective a dedicated, policy-driven manufacturing crew capable of switching instantly between industrial construction backlogs (`needs.tsv`) and 15-percent-margin commercial recipes.

Confidence in `5B`'s code and policy wiring is high: `craft-network` is verified active (`1,218` measured runs at `-10 cr/h` near-zero fuel cost), `fit-policy.json` fits `5B` properly under `"mobile"` (shields + `cargo_expander_iii` + `fuel_optimizer`, NOT empty `"bursar"`), Rule F24 reserve protection and `need_status` colour/quantity caps prevent both resource starvation and over-crafting, and wiring `load_cltv_buy_qty` unifies `craft_net.py` with the exact same anti-manipulation oracle protecting `mine_net.py`, `haul.py`, and `bursar.py` [3][5][9]. As all 10 `5B` ships commission into T2 `Subcontract` mobile workshops (`max_auto_tier: 2` active), `5B`'s primary focus is clearing industrial build kits for Ridgecrest's Resort venues (Station #1) and Stations #2-#5 (especially Dedicated Heavy Industrial & Station-Core Foundry Station #3 at `Lanternwick`/`Avior`) while converting spare cycles into high-ROIC manufactured modules and components at our active industrial hubs. Monitoring `craft-network` idle-wait ratios will immediately reveal if `6A` Logistics consolidation needs to feed more raw ore or gas into `5B` staging hubs.

As all 10 `5B` Outer Rim pilots graduate from T1 `Congregation` into T2 `Subcontract` mobile workshops, the wing becomes The Collective's dedicated construction and combat-sustainment rapid-response force [1][2]. Whenever Standing Goal 3 opens a new regional station build (stations #2 through #5) or corridor outpost fortification (outposts #4 through #8), `5B` workshops consume internally mined `3B` bulk ores and `3A`/`3D`/`3E` specialty feedstocks straight out of faction lockboxes -- turning raw minerals into `steel_plate`, `circuit_board`, `reinforced_glass`, and station kits at zero NPC purchase cost (`allow_buy_inputs_docked: false` in Logistics mode) [4][10]. Between construction waves, `craft_net.py`'s commercial lane shifts spare `5B` capacity automatically into 15-percent-margin, `>= 75 cr` profit recipes verified against real external multi-hub bids (`min_bid_qty: 5`, `load_cltv_buy_qty`) [4][5][9]. Tracking live `craft-network` completed batches per hour, green `needs.tsv` craft-backlog latency, and zero false recurring cost inflation (`book_onetime`) verifies `5B` execution continuously [3][7]. Equally important is preserving strict financial separation between Logistics construction fabrication and Trade commercial profit crafting: when `5B` produces `steel_plate` or `circuit_board` from lockbox ore, zero retail cash is spent on NPC inputs (`allow_buy_inputs_docked: false`) and the resulting industrial inventory transfers cleanly into station build kits without distorting commercial trading margins [4][5]. Together with `5A`'s 30 stationary refiners, `5B` ensures The Collective can construct new regional hubs and sustain combat fleets simultaneously while scaling toward top galactic manufacturing output.

## General Lessons

1. **Decouple dedicated manufacturing workshops from market-making desks.** Mixing stationary Exchange management (`4C`/`5A`) with deep multi-step industrial fabrication (`5B`) creates scheduling contention; explicit route exclusions keep both layers running at peak utilization [3][8].
2. **Enforce strict input-sourcing rules between commercial and industrial crafting.** Commercial crafting may profitably arbitrage cheap local ask inputs (`allow_buy_inputs_docked: true`), whereas industrial construction crafting must consume internal mined reserves (`allow_buy_inputs_docked: false`) to prevent runaway NPC cash leakage [4].
3. **Never confuse empty live API state (`{}`) with missing data (`None`).** Falling back from an empty live storage response to a stale cache file tricks autonomous crafters into attempting ghost recipes or double-buying inputs [7].
4. **Always pair crafting demand feeds with quantity caps and color-state filters.** Uncapped `need_status` text parsing drives infinite production loops long after a build kit turns green; reading explicit target quantities and skipping green needs eliminates industrial waste [5][7].
5. **Account for manufacturing raw-material purchases as inventory, not operating burn.** Failing to tag raw crafting input purchases with `book_onetime` / IAS 2 inventory accounting distorts fleet P&L massively and causes false cost-cutting alarms [7].
6. **Guard station crafting against punitive NPC fee traps.** Enforcing `max_fee_per_run_cr: 1500` and `min_wallet_keep_cr: 15000` inside `craft-policy.json` keeps autonomous workshops out of predatory high-tax NPC factories and ensures every pilot retains jump/fee working capital [4].
7. **Reject 1-unit spoof bids before computing global manufacturing demand.** Filtering out `eff_bq == 1` spikes above `guarded_bid_price(max_mult=1.50)` prevents a single manipulator's 1-unit fake buy order from redirecting ten workshops into manufacturing dead, unsellable inventory [5][9].
8. **Test every crafting consumer directly against live `pilot_of()` and storage schemas before deployment.** Unit tests running against raw manifest dicts previously masked a `dispatch.py` schema mismatch that silently disabled `craft-network`; testing live serialized pilot records eliminates silent route drop-offs permanently [7]. This verification habit prevents silent zero-ship dispatch stalls across every future manufacturing release [7].
