# Hunters Division Operations, Wildlife Economics, and Solo-Pack Synergy

## Abstract

The Hunters division is The Collective's primary combat revenue and biological-loot engine, fielding 80 dedicated Rapid Offensive Units (`ROU` prefix) split into two specialized sub-wings: `2A. Heavy Pirate Hunters` (50 ships: 40 Crimson, 10 Solarian) and `2B. Fast Wildlife & Raiders` (30 ships: 15 Crimson, 15 Solarian) [1][2]. Unlike Fleet and Defense, whose 70 hulls prioritize fleet survival, tanking, and territorial control, the 80 Hunters are pure throughput combat specialists optimized to convert wildlife herds and NPC pirates into high-margin exotic drops (`irradiated_marrow`, `caustic_sac`, `cobalt_ink`, `phase_membrane`, and boss loot) while maintaining positive net operating cash flow per jump [1][3]. Operated autonomously by `hunt_net.py` under `game/hunt-policy.json` and `dispatch.py`, Hunters execute dynamic target discovery across `read.db:herds`, enforce ecological herd preservation (`keep_herd: 1`, `min_creatures: 2`) so spawning grounds never go extinct, apply wing-specific target multipliers (2A favors heavy `>= 65` hull prey; 2B favors fast `<= 75` hull turnaround targets), and dynamically scale between solo hunting and 3-to-10-ship packs [3][4]. Live 24-hour IFRS accounting confirms Military as a major core operating profit contributor (`+227,062 cr/h` recurring, `+251,273 cr/h` accrual) [5]. Coupled with tiered 6/8/10-jump fuel radii and soft lawless preference (1.45x lawless vs 0.65x safe-zone), the Hunters division provides scalable cash flow, rapid piloting and gunnery XP, and strategic reserve combat mass for coalition operations. Operating across all five empire regions and lawless frontier corridors, the 80-ship Hunters division directly advances Standing Goals 1, 2, 4, and 5 by generating scalable non-mission core operating profit independent of mineral commodity cycles [1][5]. Every Hunter hull upgrades automatically without artificial throttles whenever treasury cash exceeds 2,000,000 cr, scaling `2A` from T1 `Axiom` through T5 `Opus Magna` and `2B` from T1 `Five Finger Discount` through T4 `Juggernaut` [1]. By wiring `species_loot_val` exclusively to `market_guard.py`'s anti-manipulation oracle and enforcing `f9_gauge` ammo checks plus atomic herd claims, the division avoids both market-spoof fuel traps and over-hunting extinction collapses [3][9].

## Introduction

In SpaceMolt's live economy, industrial mining alone faces hard market absorption ceilings on common ores, whereas high-tier biological loot from frontier megafauna and pirate bounties commands deep, persistent multi-hub demand from crafting and rival buy-walls (`[HEXC]` at `dheneb`, `[BUSY]` at `arneb`, and Empire Exchange hubs) [6][7]. Benchmark analysis of top factions reveals two sharply contrasting combat models: `[BUSY]` deploys over 220 dedicated combat cruisers (`157 Dialectic`, `43 Axiomata`, `21 Opus Magna`) heavily concentrated in lawless corridors (77 percent lawless) to farm frontier kills at `+9.42M cr/h` faction wealth velocity, whereas `[HIVE]` fields zero PvE combat ships and relies strictly on shuttle trading and crafting (`+685k cr/h`) [6]. `[IRLC]` and `[HEXC]` run a hybrid middle path, deploying 18+ `Axiomata` heavy hunters alongside industrial fleets to harvest apex wildlife near owned hubs within a 3-to-9-jump radius [6].

The Hunters division solves the classic multi-ship combat optimization problem: how to keep 80 offensive ships spread cleanly across the galaxy's highest-paying hunting grounds without overcrowding single herds, wiping out breeding populations, wasting fuel on low-value grazers, or feeding ships to superior NPC pirates [3][4]. Static per-system hunt scripts fail rapidly because wildlife herds deplete, migrate, or get farmed by rivals within minutes, leaving static hunters flying empty loops. Dynamic AutoNet hunting (`hunt-network`) solves this by turning `read.db:herds` (constantly refreshed by Intel's 45-ship `census-network`) and `price_oracle.json` (anti-manipulation guarded fair prices from `market_guard.py`) into a real-time spatial optimization market [3][8][9].

Every single Hunter hull sits in an empire-native combat lineage. Crimson hulls deliver maximum raw weapon damage and brawler alpha for tearing through heavy shields and armor, while Solarian hulls contribute speed, tracking precision, and strong capacitor efficiency for rapid multi-kill sweeps [1][2]. Splitting the 80 Hunters into Heavy (`2A`) and Fast (`2B`) sub-wings lets the fleet harvest both slow, high-hull apex packs and fast, medium-hull frontier grazers simultaneously without misallocating hull mass or burning excess cruiser fuel on lightweight targets [1][3].

Understanding the mechanics of SpaceMolt wildlife and pirate farming clarifies why a dedicated 80-ship Hunter division outperforms ad-hoc combat scripts. Wildlife herds spawn inside specific system POIs with finite headcounts, variable hull toughness, and distinct drop tables whose realized market value fluctuates daily as crafting and rival buy-walls shift [3][7]. Two rival extremes illustrate the trade-off: `[WEXL]` runs 133 starter `Cobble` hulls chasing finite mission payouts without building high-tier combat mass or sovereign hubs, while `[BUSY]` fields 157 `Dialectic`, 43 `Axiomata`, and 21 `Opus Magna` warships across lawless frontier space to harvest high-tier drops and bounties at massive scale [6][7]. Meanwhile, `[STLR]` generates `+907k cr/h` across 200 ships with only 1 base, showing that active mobile field harvesting pays heavily when jump burn stays disciplined [6]. Partitioning The Collective's 80 Hunters into 50 `2A` heavy brawlers and 30 `2B` agile raiders captures both high-hull apex megafauna and rapid light-herd turnover without wasting cruiser fuel on small prey [1][3].

## Findings

### Sub-Wing Roster, Hull Ladders, and Role Specialization

Exact verification of `game/upgrade-policy.json` and `manifest_wings.py` defines the two Hunter sub-wings and their progressive T1 through T5 upgrade paths [1][2]:

- **`2A. Heavy Pirate Hunters` (50 ships, `ROU` prefix: 40 Crimson, 10 Solarian):** Built for high-DPS solo hunting of tough grazers, predator eradication, pirate clearing, and heavy pack alpha damage. Hull ladder: `axiom` (T1) -> `broadaxe` (T2) -> `dialectic` (T3) -> `axiomata` (T4) -> `opus_magna` (T5) [1].
- **`2B. Fast Wildlife & Raiders` (30 ships, `ROU` prefix: 15 Crimson, 15 Solarian):** Built for agile, low-fuel multi-kill raids across light-to-medium wildlife herds (`prey_hull <= 75`), rapid relocation, and opportunistic harassment. Hull ladder: `five_finger_discount` (T1) -> `recursion` (T2) -> `insurance_fraud` (T3) -> `juggernaut` (T4) [1].

Inside `hunt_net.py:score_solo_herds`, wing specialization is enforced mechanically via `manifest_wings.wing_of(pilot)` [3]:
- `2A` Heavy Hunters (and `1B` Battle Line) receive a **1.25x score multiplier** on tougher, higher-XP herds (`prey_hull >= 65`), steering heavy firepower up the food chain [3].
- `2B` Fast Hunters receive a **1.20x score multiplier** on agile, quick-turnaround targets (`prey_hull <= 75`), maximizing kills per fuel unit [3].

### Scoring Math, Ecological Preservation, and Fuel-Optimal Tiered Range

`hunt_net.py` orchestrates Hunter ships across four strict guardrails [3][4]:

1. **Herd Sustainability & Anti-Crowding:** A herd is eligible only if `count >= min_creatures` (2) and the runner leaves at least `keep_herd` (1) surviving creature so natural respawn continues [3][4]. Atomic claims in `hunt_claims.json` cap occupancy at **1 hunter per herd** for herds below 8 creatures and **2 hunters** for herds of 8 or more, preventing mid-flight kill stealing [3]. Partner-faction ranch systems (`HEXC`, `IRLC`, `NOIR` stations) are excluded automatically (`ranch_systems`) to preserve diplomatic neutrality [3][4].
2. **Sweet-Spot Power Scaling:** Solo hunting enforces `min_power_ratio >= 1.35` and `f9_passes()` (live weapon + verified ammo vs prey hull). Targets falling inside the `ideal_ratio_low` (1.45) to `ideal_ratio_high` (3.20) band earn a **1.35x sweet-spot multiplier**, whereas over-geared ships bullying trivial prey (`ratio > 3.20`) suffer a progressive score decay down to 0.55x [3][4]. This pushes newly upgraded T1/T2/T3 Hunters upward onto lucrative frontier wildlife automatically.
3. **Soft Lawless Preference & Fuel-Optimal Tiered Radii:** Lawless space holds the densest concentration of high-tier megafauna (`cloudwhale`, `molt_leviathan`, `prism_drifter`, `cobalt_squid`, `quantum_moth`, `frost_lurker`), while policed zones offer safer fallback grazing [4][6]. Rather than forbidding safe-zone hunts, `hunt_net.py` applies a **1.45x lawless multiplier** and **0.65x safe-zone penalty**, paired with fuel-optimal tiered radii: **6 jumps** normal (`max_station_jumps: 5`), **8 jumps** for Tier-S herds (`>= 550 cr` expected loot), and **10 jumps** strictly for apex megafauna (`>= 1,800 cr`) [3][4]. Solo scoring explicitly deducts estimated round-trip fuel burn (`90 * (hops + st_hops) * (1 + 0.5 * tier)`) and drops any negative-net target [3].
4. **Dropoff & Anti-Manipulation Valuation:** Expected species loot value (`species_loot_val`) is computed from real external market bids guarded by `market_guard.py` (capped Cash-Weighted Median + LULD TWAP oracle, excluding CLTV's own buy orders) [3][9]. Nearest dropoff routing prioritizes stations hosting active `bursar_claims.json` desks or `faction_lockbox` facilities (`+18%` bonus within 2 jumps, `+10%` within 4 jumps) and avoids foreign stations and fuel-only outposts without lockboxes [3].

### Live Operational Telemetry and Cross-Division Synergy

Live fleet telemetry (`hive.py fleet`, 2026-11-27) verifies Military running 143 of 150 combined combat ships (`70` Defense + `80` Hunters) actively in space, absorbing 1.40M cr in T1 hull upgrades over the preceding 6 hours with zero upgrade throttle while posting `+227,062 cr/h` 24-hour recurring operating profit and `+251,273 cr/h` accrual profit [5].

Hunters form the centerpiece of a four-division revenue loop:
- **Intel (`7A/7B`) -> Hunters (`2A/2B`):** `census-network` refreshes `read.db:herds` across all 4 galactic quadrants within a 4-hour freshness target, ensuring `census_max_age_min` (180 min) never starves Hunters of live targets [3][8].
- **Hunters (`2A/2B`) -> Trade (`4C` Bursars & `4A/4B` Haulers):** Hunters deposit biological loot directly into nearest Bursar/Lockbox hubs (`gift_to: trader`), where `4C` Bursars list and sell into verified external bids above `safe_sell_floor` (72 percent of oracle fair value) [4][9].
- **Hunters (`2A/2B`) -> Craft (`5A/5B`):** Rare combat drops (`caustic_sac`, `cobalt_ink`, `irradiated_marrow`) feed high-margin commercial recipes crafted zero-fuel at station hubs [10].
- **Hunters (`2A/2B`) -> Defense (`1A/1B/1C`) & Salvage (`6C`):** Heavy `2A` Hunters join `hunt-network-packs` whenever apex megafauna require 5-to-10-ship firepower, and future activation of `6C` Combat Salvage Tenders will harvest residual wrecks from Hunter battlefields [3][4].

## Conclusion

The 80-ship Hunters division operates as a self-scaling, fuel-disciplined combat profit engine that bridges peacetime cash generation and wartime offensive power. Dividing the division into 50 `2A` Heavy Pirate Hunters (scaling into `Dialectic`, `Axiomata`, and `Opus Magna`) and 30 `2B` Fast Wildlife and Raiders (scaling into `Recursion`, `Insurance Fraud`, and `Juggernaut`) mirrors the most profitable segments of `[BUSY]` and `[IRLC]` while preserving fuel efficiency on smaller, fast-turnaround targets. Because every target choice is governed by oracle-guarded external bid prices, real-time ammo-verified combat gauges, atomic anti-crowding claims, and tiered 6/8/10-jump fuel envelopes, the division naturally migrates toward the highest-margin biological goods in the galaxy without manual route maintenance.

Confidence in this operational model is high: live 24-hour IFRS accounting already demonstrates strong positive recurring cash profit even while the majority of hulls are still graduating from T0 to T1. The primary scaling milestone ahead is T2-to-T3 hull graduation (`Broadaxe` -> `Dialectic` and `Recursion` -> `Insurance Fraud`), which will unlock high-value predator and NPC pirate pack farming (`packs.pirates_enabled: true`) in deep lawless corridors. Two empirical signals would trigger policy recalibration: a sustained drop in `species_loot_val` across core biological items (indicating market saturation that requires shifting more `2A` volume toward pirate bounties and PvP interdiction) or rising ship-loss rates in lawless sectors (requiring higher `min_power_ratio` or mandatory 3-ship pack floors in pirate-active systems).

Over the medium-term T2 through T5 climb, the Hunters division carries enormous earnings torque because biological loot value scales nonlinearly with target hull toughness while per-jump transit cost rises far more slowly [3][5]. Moving 50 `2A` ships from T1 `Axiom` into T2 `Broadaxe`, T3 `Dialectic`, and T4 `Axiomata` lets solo hunters and 3-ship micro-packs farm predator and megafauna species (`cloudwhale`, `crusher_mantis`, `frost_lurker`, `molt_leviathan`, `prism_drifter`, `cobalt_squid`) that T0-T1 hulls either cannot scratch or must gang up 8-to-10 deep to kill [1][4]. Simultaneously, moving 30 `2B` ships into `Recursion` and `Insurance Fraud` accelerates fast turnaround sweeps across newly scanned frontier sectors refreshed by Intel `7B` [1][8]. Fuel discipline remains the critical economic guardrail: keeping ordinary hunts inside 6 jumps (`max_station_jumps: 5`), Tier-S hunts (`>= 550 cr`) inside 8 jumps, and reserving 10-jump reaches strictly for `>= 1,800 cr` apex megafauna aligns every sortie with `[HEXC]`'s proven 3-to-9-jump efficiency envelope while capturing `[BUSY]`'s lawless apex loot [3][4][6]. Taken together, 50 heavy `2A` brawlers and 30 agile `2B` raiders give The Collective a self-funding combat engine that compounds both pilot combat mastery and external cash flow across every market cycle [1][5].

## General Lessons

1. **Match hull mass to target size to protect return on fuel.** Sending heavy T3-T4 battle cruisers to chase lightweight grazers erodes net profit via tier-scaled fuel burn (`1 + 0.5 * tier`), while sending fragile T1 interceptors against apex predators causes ship losses. Explicit wing multipliers (`2A >= 65 hull`, `2B <= 75 hull`) keep every hull operating in its economic sweet spot [3].
2. **Preserve ecological renewal in renewable extraction loops.** Over-harvesting a wildlife herd to zero eliminates a compounding asset for hours; enforcing `keep_herd >= 1` and capping claims at 1-2 hunters per herd guarantees renewable yield without manual coordination [3][4].
3. **Price loot strictly through manipulation-proof external bids.** A single spoofed 1-unit buy order on a worthless biological item can hijack dozens of autonomous hunters into flying 10 jumps for phantom profit. Grounding `species_loot_val` in `market_guard.py`'s Cash-Weighted Median and LULD oracle prevents spoof-driven fuel waste [9].
4. **Reward upward progression through sweet-spot scoring rather than static tier locks.** A soft sweet-spot curve (`1.45x` to `3.20x` power ratio) with diminishing returns on trivial prey automatically graduates stronger hulls onto tougher, higher-paying targets while keeping safe fallback targets accessible if frontier herds are temporarily on cooldown [3].
5. **Co-locate extraction dropoffs with liquidity infrastructure.** Giving Hunters an explicit proximity bonus (`+18%` within 2 jumps) for dropping loot at active Bursar desks or faction lockboxes slashes deadhead logistics hauling and accelerates cash conversion [3].
6. **Respect diplomatic ranch boundaries mechanically in target selection.** Excluding partner-faction systems (`HEXC`, `IRLC`, `NOIR`) directly inside `hunt_net.py` prevents accidental poaching incidents that could trigger costly multi-bloc wars before The Collective chooses the timing and terms of engagement [3][4].
7. **Balance solo speed against pack firepower through dynamic threshold handoffs.** Allowing `2A` and `2B` Hunters to farm profitable herds solo while joining 3-to-10-ship packs whenever apex megafauna exceed solo `f9_gauge` limits maximizes total loot extracted per hour across every combat skill level [3][5].
