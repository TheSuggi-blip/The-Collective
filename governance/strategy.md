# The Collective Strategy -- What the Fleet Plays For and How It Wins

## I. Purpose and Rank

This file states what The Collective plays SpaceMolt for and the way
it means to get there. It ranks beside the constitution, above every
AGENTS.md and SOUL.md: the constitution says how the fleet is
governed, this file says where the fleet is going. They cover
different ground; where a member finds them in conflict, it HALTs and
the Fleet Admiral puts the question to Suggi.

It was written by the Fleet Admiral on Suggi's instruction of
2026-11-16 from two things: what Suggi has said he wants (section II),
and what three kinds of games teach about winning a game like ours
(section III). Sections IV and V are the Fleet Admiral's reading of
both. Suggi approved the file; only his word changes it (section VII).

Every member reads it at session start (preflight). The orders in
force (`communications/orders.log`) are the next steps toward it. No
figure that changes from day to day stands here: where the fleet
stands is in the Fleet Admiral's newest report and in the fleet's own
data (the tools of the collective MCP server).

## III. What Three Kinds of Games Teach

Read on 2026-11-16; the numbers in brackets name the sources in
section VIII. A lesson is listed when it fits a game of many ships, a
player-run market and territory that can be lost.

### Real-time strategy

- The economy decides the game, and it compounds: a gap in production
  early becomes a deficit later that no skill in battle closes.[2]
  Enough workers gathering is what pays for every later choice:
  structures, more bases, an army.[1]
- Nothing stands idle: the making of workers never stops,[2] and there
  is no "good enough" state: there is always a next thing to do.[3]
- Resources are spent, not banked: a large balance is production that
  was not built.[2]
- One opening, learned until it runs by itself, frees the attention
  for scouting and reacting.[2]
- Scouting is not optional: nothing is worth more than information,
  and a move without it is a guess.[1][2]
- Advancing in technology pays in a long game and is open to attack
  while it lasts: a small defensive force comes first.[1] An army
  larger than needed starves the economy that pays for it.[1]

### Factory and base building

- Automate, then raise the rate, then rebuild: observing, expanding
  and redesigning is a key loop of play.[6]
- Plan in rates before building: start from the end product and the
  rates every line before it has to hit, and count the machines from
  the known ratios of input to output.[7]
- Build small modules that can be copied, not one large plant early;
  a tested module is saved as a blueprint and placed again.[5][7]
- A central line of the most used goods forces a planned layout and
  keeps a base from tangling.[4]
- Process near the raw material: hauling raw goods far costs more than
  hauling what was made from them.[6]
- Buffers between stages take up short interruptions, and room left
  free is what makes the next expansion possible.[7]

### Role-playing and guild games

- A group is built from roles that need each other: one takes the
  damage, one keeps the others alive, one deals it; a character made
  strong in one role gives up strength in the others.[8]
- A group shares out its gathering work so that each member goes deep
  in one craft instead of shallow in several.[9]
- Depth opens what breadth does not: the rare resources open only at
  high proficiency in one craft.[9]

### Fleets that leave safe space (EVE Online)

- Before moving out, a group answers why, where and when, and sets
  realistic goalposts on the way: members, regular fleets, a
  treasury.[10]
- In space that can be lost, keep a light footprint: no more on site
  than the work needs.[11] Channels that report hostile activity are
  standard for a group that holds space.[11]
- A group that wants to grow makes and buys from itself: its own
  market in a central system, its own miners and makers, and stock to
  replace a loss (one observer's proposal).[12] Supply chains are
  written down, and no chain hangs on one
  member.[13] Valuable cargo flies with an escort.[13]
- The soft target is the extraction, not the fortress: guarding it is
  a standing patrol, not an emergency.[14] Space that goes quiet is
  space that is easier to take.[14]

## IV. The Strategy

Ten principles. Each is one sentence a member can test a plan against.

1. **The economy comes first, and it is reinvested.** Steady income is
   the measure. Credits that lie in wallets above the reserve are idle
   workers: they go, in this order, into the tool that opens paying
   work, into what lifts the present bottleneck, into upgrades with a
   measured gain, into the reserve for a base, and into new ships.
2. **No ship idle, no work at a loss.** A plan is a standing loop that
   holds paying work for every ship of its division for the hours
   between two turns. A ship without paying work is docked, costs
   nothing, and is a question its Admiral answers at once.
3. **Find the bottleneck, lift it, find the next.** What limits income
   is one thing at a time: a tool, the depth of a bid, a field, a
   hold, a level. The fleet's data names it; the turn's work goes
   there first.
4. **Scout always; decide on what was measured.** Every reading
   becomes a file every member can ask, by itself. An estimate is
   tested with one ship before a division follows it.
5. **What works is a blueprint.** A route file is our build order. A
   route that paid is copied to as many ships as its place carries; a
   new ship flies the same opening as the ones before it: the missions
   that pay once, the tool of its job, then a paying route.
6. **Roles in harmony.** Miners, haulers and traders earn. Scouts see,
   and hunt on the way. Drones guard, escort and hunt. Each ship is
   fitted and levelled for its own job, toward the next step that
   opens a better hull.
7. **Grow by capacity.** A division grows when it shows paying places
   for more ships than it has. The fleet expands only to what it can
   supply and guard.
8. **Build the chain inward.** Mine, haul, make, then sell or use: the
   more steps our own ships do, the less a market can take from us.
   Shared storage is our central line; the first base stands beside
   the best field.
9. **Hold the stance.** We start no fight. A ship that is attacked
   defends or flees; its escort may fire to cover it, then both
   escape. Miners and haulers beyond the border are the soft targets:
   they are guarded and watched. Beyond the border the footprint is
   light. Diplomacy has one voice.
10. **Learn, then automate.** Every turn leaves a measured result, a
    lesson in a knowledge file and, where a script can do the work
    next time, the script. What a script can do, no member does by
    hand.
11. **Beat #1 [HEXC] & #1 [BUSY] by compounding capital per ship and
    eliminating empty flight.** Raw ship count loses to high-tier hulls,
    multi-slot fittings, zero-fuel docked factory crafting, **Station
    Bursars** (designated hub traders maintaining standing buy/sell
    orders and `Notice Board` procurement contracts logged in
    `communications/contracts.log` instead of flying `-500 cr/h` empty
    jump loops), **selective gangue jettisoning** on deep scarce-ore
    belts (`mine.py --jettison-gangue`), **wormhole shortcut mapping**,
    **opportunistic fleet sensing** (cutting redundant home census
    burn), **frontier fuel toll-booths & station tourism appeal** on our
    sovereign stations, and strict **CaMeL + Dynamic Spotlighting**
    quarantine of all external player messages (`comms_guard.py`).
12. **Manufacturing & trade powerhouse, maximal territorial reach, and
    cross-empire hull min-maxing.** Optimize steady income/hour, crafted
    manufacturing output, multi-hub trade volume, resource extraction, and
    Consolidated Net Worth across the entire organization as one enterprise
    -- never sub-optimizing per siloed division or turning off core engines
    instead of fixing caps. Place our hard-capped stations (`5`, with
    faction shipyards and licensed multi-empire blueprints) and outposts
    (`8`) at high-centrality hubs and choke points. Because SpaceMolt
    applies zero penalty when flying another empire's hull, standardize
    best-in-class hulls by job across all five empires (using Piloting-0
    skips and auto listed-ship buys) while pairing each pilot's native
    empire Mastery skill (`F35`) to its division role (`O2`, `O4`).

What the fleet does not do: work at a loss to look busy; mine for
skill alone; grow ahead of its income; stake a division on one route,
one buyer or one station; fire first; speak to another player without
Suggi's word.

## V. The Phases

The Fleet Admiral's reading of the way, with a goalpost for each
phase. The fleet works on one phase and prepares the next. Suggi
decides when a phase is reached and what each next step may cost.

| Phase | What it is | Reached when |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1. Income | Paying routes in every division; the tool of its job on every ship that earns; no ship idle | The fleet's steady net is above zero over 24 hours, and at every turn of a day no ship waits while paying work stands open |
| 2. Chain and faction | Shared faction storage; one chain from field to finished good flown by our own ships; the first better hulls where they pay | A chain measured to pay more than selling the raw good, and income that covers the weekly tax and the reserve for a base |
| 3. First base | An outpost beside the best field beyond the border: storage and a market of our own, supplied by our chain, guarded by Military, watched by Intel | It has stood a week, and the routes around it earn more than before it stood |
| 4. Station and armada | A station of our own, ships built for war, space we hold | Suggi's word |

Whether the faction leaves the Voidborn empire is Suggi's decision and
cannot be undone; nothing in phases 1 to 3 needs it.

## VI. How It Is Used

- The Fleet Admiral's orders name the phase the fleet is in and the
  bottleneck of the turn, and her report says how far the goalpost is.
- An Admiral tests its plan against section IV before a turn ends:
  which principle does each route serve, and which ship holds no work.
- A proposal that breaks a principle is put to Suggi with its reason;
  it is never carried out first.
